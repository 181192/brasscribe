• Onset F1 and its tolerance

    A written note is right when a played note has the same pitch and starts within the tolerance: 50 ms is the usual choice, and we also report 100 ms because a soft brass attack speaks late. Precision is the share of the written notes that are right, recall the share of the played notes that were found, and F1 combines the two. Note ends are ignored unless a figure says otherwise.

    F1 = 2 · precision · recall / (precision + recall)

  • Position in the bar and within the beat

    Of the notes that were matched, the share whose written position is exactly the score's. In the bar needs the bar lines to be right. Within the beat only needs the note on the right subdivision, so it survives a shifted bar line. Written length is counted the same way: exact, or wrong.

  • Written F1

    The strictest measure, and the one a reader feels: a note counts only when it is heard (pitch and onset within 100 ms) and sits where the score has it in the bar. Metre, bar phase and the grid all have to be right at once.

  • Metre

    The share of parts whose written bar is as long, in quarter notes, as the score's. A part in three written in four fails, whatever its notes. A tracker that counts twice as fast or half as fast is not punished for that here.

  • Doubt marks: recall and precision

    A ?question mark is useful when it sits on a wrong note. Recall is the share of the wrong notes that carry a mark; precision is the share of the marks that sit on a wrong note. In the drawing three notes are wrong and two are marked, one of them rightly: recall 1 of 3, precision 1 of 2.
