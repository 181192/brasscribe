Research paper

Fretscribe: Transcribing Recordings into Tablature with Pretrained Models and a Fingering Search

Kristoffer-Andre Kalliainen · The Fretscribe project · Built {{built}}; every number and figure is read from the repository's benchmark file at that commit · Companion paper: Brasscribe (brass-band scores)

Abstract

We describe and evaluate Fretscribe, a system that transcribes recordings into tablature for bass, guitar, ukulele and mandolin. Pretrained models for source separation, beat tracking, note transcription and fundamental-frequency estimation are used without further training; deterministic rules clean and quantise the notes, and a shortest-path search assigns a string and a fret to every note. The system is evaluated with note-level precision, recall and F-measure at the ±50 ms onset tolerance of mir_eval, with string agreement against annotated strings, and with tempo and metre accuracy. On {{data fretted/bass-tab/song.tracks int}} synthesised songs a bass line reaches an onset F-measure of {{span fretted/bass-tab/song.onset_f1 fretted/bass-tab/instrument.onset_f1}}. On GuitarSet, for three players that were not used to tune the rules, a guitar recorded alone reaches {{span fretted/guitar-tab/report.solo.onset_f1 fretted/guitar-tab/report.comp.onset_f1}}; a guitar within a synthesised song reaches {{data fretted/guitar-tab/song.onset_f1}}. Of the correctly detected notes of single-line guitar playing, {{data fretted/guitar-tab/report.solo.string_agreement pct}} are assigned to the string the player used, and {{data fretted/guitar-tab/report.comp.string_agreement pct}} in chordal playing. The tempo is correct for {{span fretted/bass-tab/song.tempo_ok fretted/bass-tab/instrument.tempo_ok fretted/guitar-tab/report.solo.tempo_ok fretted/guitar-tab/report.comp.tempo_ok fretted/guitar-tab/song.tempo_ok pct}} of the bass and guitar recordings. The confidence marks have a precision of {{span fretted/bass-tab/song.doubt_precision fretted/bass-tab/instrument.doubt_precision fretted/guitar-tab/report.solo.doubt_precision fretted/guitar-tab/report.comp.doubt_precision fretted/guitar-tab/song.doubt_precision pct}} and a recall of {{span fretted/bass-tab/song.doubt_recall fretted/bass-tab/instrument.doubt_recall fretted/guitar-tab/report.solo.doubt_recall fretted/guitar-tab/report.comp.doubt_recall fretted/guitar-tab/song.doubt_recall pct}}. All tablatures produced were playable. The evidence for bass is synthesised, that for ukulele and mandolin consists of a few rendered passages, no benchmark runs in continuous integration, and nothing was measured on recordings made with a phone.

Keywords: automatic music transcription, guitar tablature, fingering, string assignment, source separation, evaluation.

Plain-language summary: Fretscribe hears a bass line well and a guitar on its own fairly well. A guitar inside a full song is mostly beyond it. The frets it writes are playable, but often not the ones the player used, and its question marks catch a minority of the mistakes.

1 Introduction

Tablature shows where a note is played on a fretted instrument: one line per string and a fret number on it. Transcribing tablature from audio adds two problems to automatic music transcription (AMT) {{cite benetos2019amt}}. First, a pitch can usually be played at several positions on the neck, and a microphone recording does not identify the one that was used. Second, the instrument is rarely alone: within a song it is mixed with voice, percussion and other instruments, and a guitar plays chords whose notes overlap.

Fretscribe shares its listening models and its rules for rhythm with Brasscribe, which is described in a companion paper. It runs on the user's own devices; every tablature is produced on the user's computer, and the phone app carries no listening models. This paper covers what is specific to tablature. {{ref fig:tab-notes}} summarises note detection for each instrument, alone and within a song.

{{chart tab-notes}}

The contributions of this paper are:

  • a description of an audio-to-tablature pipeline built from pretrained models, deterministic note rules and a fingering search (Section 3);
  • an evaluation on real guitar recordings with annotated strings, with a separation between the players used to tune the rules and those used to report results, and on synthesised material for bass, ukulele and mandolin (Sections 4 and 5);
  • an analysis of the errors and of the threats to validity, including a case in which the tuned and the reported players differ markedly (Sections 5.6 and 6).

2 Related work

Note transcription and f0 estimation. Basic Pitch is a lightweight, instrument-agnostic note transcriber {{cite bittner2022basicpitch}}; SwiftF0 estimates the fundamental frequency (f0) of a monophonic line {{cite nieradzik2025swiftf0}}. Beat This! tracks beats and downbeats {{cite foscarin2024beatthis}}, and a band-split RoPE Transformer (BS-RoFormer) {{cite lu2024bsroformer}} separates a mixture into stems.

Tablature transcription. Kehling et al. estimate string and fret together with score parameters for electric guitar {{cite kehling2014idmt}}. Wiggins and Kim estimate tablature frame by frame with a convolutional network (TabCNN) and define tablature precision, recall and a tablature disambiguation rate {{cite wiggins2019tabcnn}}; GuitarSet provides recordings with a pickup per string, so that every note has an annotated string {{cite xi2018guitarset}}. In contrast to these learned approaches, Fretscribe does not estimate the string from the audio: it detects notes and then chooses positions by a search over playable fingerings.

Gap. We found no annotated recordings of ukulele or mandolin, and no dataset of bass recordings with aligned notes that we could use; the evaluation for those instruments therefore rests on synthesised audio (Section 4.1).

3 Method

{{ref fig:fret-pipeline}} shows the pipeline.

{{pipeline fret-pipeline}}

3.1 Listening models

BS-RoFormer SW {{cite audioseparator}} separates a song into stems, and the stem of the instrument is analysed. It has stems for bass and for guitar and none for ukulele or mandolin, which are read from the guitar stem; the step is omitted when the recording contains the instrument alone. Beat This! provides beats and downbeats. Basic Pitch provides the notes, and SwiftF0 a second estimate that is used only where one note sounds at a time.

3.2 From notes to quantised notes

Rules remove overtones that the note model reported as notes, discard notes outside the range of the instrument and, for a bass, test whether a line detected an octave too high was played there. The notes are then quantised to the beat grid with written durations by the rules described in the companion paper. Each note then receives a confidence between 0 and 1. It is set by rule, not by a fitted model as in the companion paper: 0.5 plus half of the amplitude that Basic Pitch reports for the note. A note is placed in doubt, and marked with a ?question mark, when its amplitude is low and, in a monophonic line, SwiftF0 did not detect it at that pitch; or, within a chord, when its amplitude is low, or moderately low and the pitch is not detected again within a short time. The thresholds were set by hand.

3.3 Fingering

The fingering is a search and not a learned model. Notes that start together are placed together, each on its own string, within the span of a hand. Each candidate position has a cost: the stretch of the fingers, the height of the hand on the neck, and the distance the hand moves from the preceding notes, measured in millimetres along the neck, so that the same rule applies to a bass and a ukulele. The path of least total cost through the passage, found by dynamic programming, is the tablature. Open strings and known chord shapes also enter the cost. The search is deterministic, and a separate check confirms that every position is playable. A note that cannot be fingered together with the others is omitted until the tablature is playable, so playability holds by construction: {{span fretted/guitar-tab/report.solo.unplayable_dropped fretted/guitar-tab/report.comp.unplayable_dropped pct}} of the notes are omitted on GuitarSet, and {{span fretted/ukulele-tab/ukulele_high_g_heldout_dev.instrument.unplayable_dropped fretted/ukulele-tab/ukulele_high_g_heldout_dev.song.unplayable_dropped fretted/ukulele-tab/ukulele_low_g_heldout_dev.instrument.unplayable_dropped fretted/ukulele-tab/ukulele_low_g_heldout_dev.song.unplayable_dropped fretted/ukulele-tab/ukulele_baritone_heldout_dev.instrument.unplayable_dropped fretted/ukulele-tab/ukulele_baritone_heldout_dev.song.unplayable_dropped fretted/mandolin-tab/mandolin_heldout_dev.instrument.unplayable_dropped fretted/mandolin-tab/mandolin_heldout_dev.song.unplayable_dropped pct}} on the rendered strummed passages for ukulele and mandolin. Tuning, capo and playing style are parameters of this stage only, so a different tuning does not require the audio to be analysed again.

4.2 Splits and data leakage

No model was trained. The rules have thresholds; for guitar these were chosen on three of the six GuitarSet players and results are reported for the other three, so that a threshold is not evaluated on the recordings on which it was chosen. Section 5.6 shows the value of that separation. For bass there is no such split: the Slakh set is development data, and one threshold of the octave test was chosen on the set that scores it. The rendered passages have held-out groups, with exceptions that eval/README.md lists rule by rule.

4.3 Metrics

All measures lie between 0 and 1; higher is better. Note-level measures are computed with mir_eval.transcription, onset-only, and all of them use the standard ±50 ms onset tolerance. An octave error is a reference note that remains unmatched while an estimated note one or two octaves away starts within the tolerance.

    {{part measure-onset}} {{part measure-string}} {{part measure-doubt}} {{part measure-metre}}

In addition we report tempo accuracy, the share of recordings whose estimated tempo is within 4 % of the reference tempo; chord precision and recall, {{ref eq:prf}} restricted to notes that start together in groups of three or more; and playability, which is a requirement and not a score: a tablature with an unplayable position fails the benchmark.

4.4 Protocol

Model outputs are computed once and stored; scribe bench rescores each suite and fails when a value has moved by more than the tolerance in eval/baselines.json. Results are means over recordings. Per-recording values are not stored for the tablature suites, so no dispersion or confidence interval is reported, and no significance tests were carried out; differences are reported descriptively. None of the tablature suites runs in continuous integration, because they require data or models that are not in the repository; they are run on one computer.

5.5 A bass without its low frequencies

Small microphones pass little of the lowest octave of a bass. {{ref fig:tab-phone}} simulates this by high-pass filtering the bass before analysis. 6 Discussion

6.1 Error analysis

  • An instrument within a song. A guitar in a mixture reaches F = {{data fretted/guitar-tab/song.onset_f1}}, with a chord recall of {{data fretted/guitar-tab/song.chord_recall pct}}. The value is no higher for songs with a single guitar ({{data fretted/guitar-tab/song_one.onset_f1}}).{{holds fretted/guitar-tab/song_one.onset_f1 < 0.5}}
  • String assignment. Fewer than half of the correctly detected notes of a single line are on the player's string (Section 5.2).
  • Octave errors are infrequent for a bass within a song ({{data fretted/bass-tab/song.octave_err_rate pct}} of reference notes) and more frequent at the bottom of a five-string bass ({{span fretted/bass-tab/low_b.song.octave_err_rate fretted/bass-tab/low_b.instrument.octave_err_rate pct}}) and when the low frequencies are missing (Section 5.5). For guitar chords the rate is {{data fretted/guitar-tab/report.comp.octave_err_rate pct}}.
  • Chords. In chordal playing the chord recall is {{data fretted/guitar-tab/report.comp.chord_recall pct}} and the chord precision {{data fretted/guitar-tab/report.comp.chord_precision pct}}.
  • Tempo is wrong for up to four recordings in ten (Section 5.3).
  • Tremolo. A mandolin melody in fast repeated notes reaches F = {{span fretted/mandolin-tab/mandolin_tremolo.instrument.onset_f1 fretted/mandolin-tab/mandolin_heldout_tremolo.instrument.onset_f1}} alone, and {{data fretted/mandolin-tab/mandolin_tremolo.song.onset_f1}} and {{data fretted/mandolin-tab/mandolin_heldout_tremolo.song.onset_f1}} within a song, on one rendered passage each.
  • Notes removed in cleaning. The overtones and low-amplitude notes that the cleaning removes amount to {{span fretted/mandolin-tab/mandolin_tremolo.instrument.leftovers_dropped fretted/mandolin-tab/mandolin_tremolo.song.leftovers_dropped pct}} of the written notes in the tremolo passages and {{span fretted/guitar-tab/idmt.tune.leftovers_dropped fretted/guitar-tab/idmt.report.leftovers_dropped pct}} on IDMT-SMT-Guitar, against {{span fretted/guitar-tab/report.solo.leftovers_dropped fretted/guitar-tab/report.comp.leftovers_dropped pct}} for the reported GuitarSet players.

6.2 Threats to validity

  • Synthesised audio. All bass results, and all results for ukulele and mandolin, come from synthesised or rendered audio; the latter from one sampled instrument each and a few passages.
  • Tuning on the test data. Only the guitar rules were tuned on players other than those reported (Section 4.2).
  • No dispersion. Results are means without a measure of spread (Section 4.4).
  • Comparability. The string measures are note-level and are not comparable with frame-level tablature metrics in the literature; the ±50 ms tolerance used here differs from the ±100 ms headline tolerance of the companion paper.
  • Simulation. The microphone experiment is a filter, not a recording.

7 Limitations

  • No recording of a real bass, ukulele or mandolin with reference notes was evaluated.
  • No recording made with a phone was evaluated.
  • No tablature benchmark runs in continuous integration; the values are gated only when the benchmark is run on a computer that holds the data.
  • String agreement is known for guitar only, because only the guitar sets annotate the string.
  • The confidence marks cover pitch and onset only, not the string, the rhythm or the tempo.
  • Playing techniques such as bends, slides and hammer-ons are not evaluated.
  • Nearly all recordings are in quadruple time; other metres rest on {{data fretted/meter-tab/excerpts int}} rendered passages.
  • The computational cost of a tablature job was not measured; the models are those of the companion paper, which reports their cost on one laptop.

8 Conclusion and future work

With pretrained models and deterministic rules, a bass line and a guitar recorded alone are transcribed well enough to serve as a draft, whereas a guitar within a song is not. The fingering search always yields playable tablature, but for single lines it agrees with the player's choice of string for fewer than half of the notes. The confidence marks are precise and have low recall.

Future work includes recordings of real basses, ukuleles and mandolins with reference notes, and recordings made with phones; transcription of a guitar within a mixture; a fingering cost that reflects how guitarists choose positions, evaluated against the annotated strings of GuitarSet; a tablature benchmark in continuous integration on redistributable data; and measurements of computational cost.

9 Reproducibility statement

  • Code. The system, the benchmarks and this page are in the public repository github.com/181192/brasscribe; the page was built from the commit named under the title.
  • Commands. pixi run scribe bench fretted rescores the tablature suites from stored model outputs and compares them with eval/baselines.json; eval/README.md gives the commands that build each set and run the models. site/build.sh builds this page and fails if a stated finding no longer holds.
  • Reproducible from the repository alone. None of the results: no tablature suite runs in continuous integration, and every suite needs a dataset or model outputs that are not in the repository. The page itself can be rebuilt from the stored baselines.
  • Requiring a local download. All results: GuitarSet, IDMT-SMT-Guitar and Slakh must be downloaded and the models run locally; the rendered passages are generated locally with FluidSynth and the SoundFont.
  • Data availability. GuitarSet, IDMT-SMT-Guitar and Slakh are available from their publishers. The rendered passages are generated by scripts in the repository.
  • Method. The fingering search is documented in core/targets/fretted/README.md.

10 Ethics and licence statement

  • Model licences. Basic Pitch is Apache-2.0; SwiftF0 and Beat This! are MIT. No licence is stated for the BS-RoFormer SW weights; they are downloaded by the user's computer from their publisher and are not redistributed by the project.
  • Dataset licences are given in {{ref table:tabdata}}. IDMT-SMT-Guitar is licensed for non-commercial use without derivatives; it is used for evaluation only and nothing derived from it is distributed.
  • Privacy. No recording, and no tablature derived from one, is sent to a server.
  • Telemetry. ONNX Runtime, the Microsoft library that executes models in parts of the engine, reports its own usage to Microsoft with a device identifier; THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md documents what is sent.
