From recording to tab, one question at a time.

Fretscribe asks one thing at a time and tells you what it is doing. Nothing is written as frets until you have seen what it heard.

  1. {{shot home sizes="(max-width: 520px) 44vw, 232px" alt="Home: Open a recording, Record with the microphone, Record what's playing and Open a tab." load="lazy"}}

    Open or record

    Open a recording you have, play into the microphone, or record what another app on the phone is playing.

  2. {{shot what-is-this sizes="(max-width: 520px) 44vw, 232px" alt="What is this? for Bass line.wav, with two choices: Just my instrument, a recording of the bass alone, and A full song, a band or a record where Fretscribe picks out the bass." load="lazy"}}

    What is this?

    Just your instrument, or a full song where Fretscribe picks out your part. Your answer decides how it listens.

  3. {{shot writing sizes="(max-width: 520px) 44vw, 232px" alt="Writing down the notes: step 2 of 6, Listening for the beat, with every step listed, from Sending the recording to your computer to Laying out the tab, and Cancel." load="lazy"}}

    Writing down the notes

    Each step in plain words: the beat, the notes, the bars, then strings and frets. Your computer does the listening.

  4. {{shot check sizes="(max-width: 520px) 44vw, 232px" alt="Check the song: Tuning, sounds like Drop D, written for Standard, with Use Drop D. Octave, written one octave lower than it was heard.     Check the song

    The tuning it heard, the octave, the key and the tempo. If one of these is wrong, every fret is wrong, so you see them first.

Read it, then play along.

The tab is yours to practise with, not just to look at.

  • The player follows the tab bar by bar and shows the beat you are on.
  • Mute my part takes your instrument out, so you play it yourself.
  • Practice slows the recording down and repeats the hard bars.
  • Music stand fills the screen with the tab, for when the phone sits on an amp.
  • A note Fretscribe was not sure about gets a ?. It does not pretend.

Written for your instrument.

The same notes sit in different places on a bass, a seven-string or a ukulele. Fretscribe asks once what you play and writes every tab for that.

  • Guitar, bass, ukulele and mandolin, with the number of strings you have.
  • Your usual tuning: standard, E-flat, D or C standard, Drop D, C or B, DADGAD, Open G, D or E.
  • Left-handed instruments, and instruments played upside down.
  • When a recording sounds like another tuning, Check the song says so and offers it.

Your phone, and your computer.

Hearing single notes in a recording is heavy work. Bandroom, a free program on your own Mac or PC, does it for the phone over your own Wi-Fi. The recording goes from your phone to your computer and nowhere else: no account, nothing online.

Bandroom is the same program Brasscribe uses, so one computer serves both apps.
